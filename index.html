Overview —
a two-week immersion in Sanskrit Computational Linguistics frameworks in the age of AI.

This summer school introduces participants to the emerging interdisciplinary domain that integrates Indian grammatical theories with modern computational techniques. The major focus of the summer school is to initiate participants in building prototypes or small modules to solve contemporary, relevant problems in the field of Sanskrit Computational Linguistics. There will be lectures by eminent scholars and researchers in the field, providing sufficient background on the Indian Grammatical Tradition relevant to computational linguistics, an introduction to Python, and modern AI tools to carry out the projects.

About the School

Tradition, formalised — computation, contextualised.

Sanskrit has, for over two millennia, been the site of one of humanity's most rigorous traditions of formal linguistic description. From Pāṇini's Aṣṭādhyāyī to the Navya-Nyāya philosophers, the language has been examined with a precision that anticipates much of what modern computational linguistics seeks to formalise.

This summer school is designed to bring together two communities that rarely share a room — Sanskrit scholars trained in the traditional śāstra, and researchers in NLP, computational linguistics, and digital humanities. Over two weeks, participants will engage with morphological analysers, dependency parsers, OCR for manuscripts, and the application of modern language models to classical Sanskrit texts.

The school is hosted by the Centre of Digital Humanities at Manipal Academy of Higher Education, Bengaluru, in academic association with IIT Kharagpur's ongoing initiatives in Indic NLP and digital philology.
